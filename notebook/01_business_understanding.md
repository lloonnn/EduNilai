## 2. Background

Malaysia produces hundreds of thousands of university graduates annually. Yet graduate unemployment stays high, starting salaries have barely moved in real terms, and PTPTN debt is growing. The assumption that a degree is always worth it is increasingly being questioned — by students, by families, and by the data.

EduNilai investigates whether a Malaysian university degree is still a sound financial investment, for whom it works, and for whom it no longer does.

---

## 3. Problem Statement

> Malaysia's higher education system is expanding in scale while declining in financial return. Rising tuition costs, stagnating graduate salaries, and growing inequality in outcomes mean that a university degree is no longer a guaranteed investment — and for some Malaysians, it may already be a losing one.

---

## 4. The Six Problem Dimensions

**① The Confidence Crisis**
Public trust in higher education is falling. Is that reaction justified by the data?

**② The Cost-Wage Squeeze**
Tuition fees are rising. Graduate salaries are not keeping pace. The wage premium of a degree is shrinking.

**③ The Risk of Negative ROI**
For certain fields and institution types, the lifetime financial return of a degree is now lower than never attending university at all.

**④ The Subject Divide**
STEM and professional degrees retain strong returns. Arts, humanities, and general business degrees are increasingly difficult to justify financially.

**⑤ The Demographic Gap**
The same degree does not return the same value to everyone. Gender and ethnicity create measurable differences in what graduates actually earn.

**⑥ The Incompletion Tax** *(data permitting)*
Dropping out or graduating late eliminates the financial benefit of a degree while keeping the full cost.

---

## 5. Objectives

**Business Objectives**
- Give students and families honest, data-driven information to make better education decisions
- Identify exactly where Malaysia's education-to-employment pipeline is failing
- Quantify how demographic background affects graduate financial outcomes
- Produce specific, actionable policy recommendations backed by evidence

**Analytical Objectives**
- Calculate real tuition costs by field and institution type, 2010–2025 (inflation-adjusted)
- Calculate real graduate starting salaries and the college wage premium over the same period
- Compute ROI, Net Present Value, and payback period by field of study
- Disaggregate findings by gender and ethnicity
- Benchmark Malaysia against Singapore and at least one other ASEAN country
- Forecast salary and cost trajectories 5 years beyond 2025
- Build a composite ROI Score ranking all major fields of study

---

## 6. Scope

**In scope**
- Public universities, private universities, polytechnics, community colleges
- All major fields with available Graduate Tracer Study data
- Undergraduate degrees only
- Demographic disaggregation by gender and ethnicity

**Out of scope**
- Non-financial returns of education (personal growth, civic value)
- Postgraduate degrees
- International students or Malaysians studying abroad

---

## 7. Key Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | What They Need From EduNilai |
|---|---|
| Students & families | Which degree gives the best financial return |
| Ministry of Higher Education | Where intake quotas and curriculum need reform |
| PTPTN | Whether graduates can realistically repay loans |
| Universities | Which programmes are delivering poor outcomes |
| Ministry of Economy | Whether education spend is generating national returns |
| Employers | Whether degree qualifications signal real productivity |

---

## 8. Assumptions

- A working lifetime of 40 years from graduation is used for lifetime earnings calculations
- A discount rate of 4% per annum is applied for NPV calculations
- DOSM CPI is used for all inflation adjustments
- Graduate Tracer Study data is treated as representative of the broader graduate population
- The wage gap between graduates and SPM leavers is used as a proxy for the financial return attributable to the degree

---

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Data gaps for 2010–2015 in Graduate Tracer Study | Supplement with DOSM Labour Force Survey; note gaps as limitations |
| Dropout rate data unavailable | Exclude Dimension 6 if insufficient data; report as limitation |
| Inconsistent tuition fee data across institution types | Use PTPTN average loan amounts as proxy |
| Team members using inconsistent datasets | Pre-agree on shared dataset list and variable definitions before individual analysis begins |

---

## 10. Success Criteria

The project succeeds if it delivers all of the following:

- ✅ ROI calculated for at least 8 major fields across public and private institutions
- ✅ At least one field identified with negative ROI or payback period exceeding 15 years
- ✅ Demographic disparity quantified by gender and at least one ethnic group
- ✅ 5-year ROI forecast produced for top 5 and bottom 5 fields
- ✅ Three specific, data-backed policy recommendations delivered
- ✅ All four submission components consistent in numbers, findings, and narrative
