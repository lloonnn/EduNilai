# 🎓 EduNilai: ROI of University Degree in Malaysia (2010–2025)
### *"You paid for a degree. Did it pay you back?"*

---

## What Is EduNilai?

Every year, hundreds of thousands of Malaysian families make one of the biggest financial decisions of their lives — sending their children to university. They take out PTPTN loans, skip holidays, and work extra hours, all based on one shared belief: *a degree is worth it.*

But is it still true?

EduNilai is a data-driven project that asks the question Malaysian families are already asking at dinner tables — but nobody has answered with real numbers. We go beyond opinions and assumptions to calculate the actual **Return on Investment (ROI)** of a Malaysian university degree, tracking 15 years of data from 2010 to 2025.

What we found will surprise you.

---

## The Problem We Are Investigating

Imagine spending RM60,000 and four years of your life on a degree, be it at public or private university — only to graduate into a job that pays RM2,000 a month. Meanwhile, your friend who went straight into the workforce after SPM has been earning, saving, and building skills for four years already.

Who made the better financial decision?

For a long time, the answer was obvious: the graduate always wins in the long run. But that assumption is increasingly under pressure. Tuition fees have risen sharply. Graduate salaries have barely moved. And the gap between what a degree costs and what it actually returns is closing — or in some cases, has already reversed.

EduNilai investigates six dimensions of this crisis:

**1. The Confidence Crisis**
Malaysians are losing faith in higher education — but are they wrong to?

**2. The Cost-Wage Squeeze**
Fees are going up. Salaries are not keeping up. The math is getting worse every year.

**3. The Risk of Negative Returns**
For certain courses and certain students, a degree now costs more — financially — than never going to university at all.

**4. The Subject Divide**
Not all degrees are created equal. The field you choose matters enormously — and some fields have become financial traps.

**5. The Demographic Gap**
The same degree does not return the same value for everyone. Gender and ethnicity create significant differences in what graduates actually earn.

**6. The Incompletion Tax**
Dropping out or taking too long to graduate does not just delay your career — it can financially set you back for a decade.

## Why This Matters

This is not an academic exercise. The findings of EduNilai have direct implications for:

- **Students and families** deciding whether, where, and what to study
- **Policymakers** allocating education budgets and setting intake quotas
- **Universities** designing programmes that actually serve their students' futures
- **Employers** understanding the real value they are receiving — and offering

Higher education is Malaysia's single largest investment in human capital. EduNilai exists to ask whether that investment is paying off — and to show, clearly and honestly, where it is not.

## The Data Behind EduNilai

All data used in EduNilai comes from official Malaysian and ASEAN government sources, ensuring accuracy, credibility, and compliance with research standards. Primary sources include the MOHE Graduate Tracer Study, DOSM Labour Force and Salary Surveys, PTPTN Annual Reports, and the DOSM Consumer Price Index, covering the period 2010 to 2025.

No opinions. No assumptions. Just the numbers — and what they tell us.
